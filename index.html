About

Green materials begin with green manufacturing.

Aqueous plasma electrolysis and synthesis at room temperature put the focus on how materials are made, with the goal of reducing chemical demands, using energy wisely and making sustainable production more practical.

Research

Three threads, one way of thinking.

All three share a belief. If you can see a material forming and working, you can design it better.

01

Multilayer functional membranes and optical diagnostics

Layered oxide coatings are formed on titanium through plasma electrolytic oxidation. Their composition and structure can be adjusted through the electrolyte and electrical conditions, creating functional surfaces for catalysis and energy applications.

Optical emission spectroscopy follows the plasma discharges during fabrication. Combined with microscopy and electrochemical measurements, these observations help connect what happens during growth with the structure and performance of the finished material.

Plasma electrolytic oxidation Optical emission spectroscopy Functional oxide coatings
02

Microfluidic nanoparticle synthesis at room temperature

Microfluidic reactors provide a controlled environment for growing nanoparticles in continuous flow. Flow rates, reagent concentrations and residence time offer ways to adjust particle size and shape under mild conditions.

Current work focuses on copper oxide nanoparticles, combining optical monitoring with electron microscopy and image analysis to understand how different shapes develop. The aim is to make synthesis more reproducible and establish reliable routes to particles for catalysis and further chemical conversion.

Microfluidics Nanoparticle growth Shape control
03

Lab automation and data guided synthesis

Automated platforms bring synthesis, reaction monitoring and data collection into a connected workflow. Routine steps become easier to repeat, while consistent records make it possible to compare experiments and identify useful trends.

Ongoing development combines optical measurements, image analysis and modelling to guide the choice of experimental conditions. The goal is a system that learns from each run and helps researchers decide what to test next.

Automated synthesis Optical monitoring Modelling
